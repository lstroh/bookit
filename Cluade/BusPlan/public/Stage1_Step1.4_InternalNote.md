# Stage 1 — Step 1.4: Internal Note
## Wimbledon Smart Business — Complete Step-by-Step Operational Guide

**Document Version:** 1.0
**Date:** March 2026
**Status:** Active Reference
**Applies To:** Immediately after every discovery call

---

## What This Step Is

Step 1.4 is what happens in the five minutes immediately after you hang up the Zoom call. It is not glamorous, but it is the step most solo operators skip — and then regret when they are writing a proposal three days later and can't remember whether the client said they had a logo or whether they needed one designed.

The goal is simple: while the call is still fresh and Jamie's summary is in front of you, get everything logged in Bonsai so that nothing is lost and the next step is already scheduled.

This is also the moment you make a first honest assessment: is this a genuine prospect worth a proposal, or did something on the call change your view?

---

## Timing

Do this **before** you do anything else after the call. Not after lunch. Not after your next meeting. Now.

Jamie's summary arrives within a few minutes of the call ending — use it as your source rather than relying on memory. Cross-reference it against the questions in the call guide to make sure nothing is missing.

---

## Step 1 — Review the Jamie Summary (2 minutes)

Open Jamie and review the auto-generated summary. Check:

- Is the key pain point captured clearly?
- Are the booking specifics there — staff count, services, deposits, payment model?
- Is content readiness noted — logo, photos, copy?
- Is the agreed next step recorded?

If anything is missing or unclear, add it as a manual note in Jamie before you close it. You will not remember it later.

---

## Step 2 — Update Bonsai (3 minutes)

Open the prospect's record in Bonsai and fill in the call summary. Copy this template into the notes field of every prospect record at the point of first contact — then fill it in after the call.

```
CALL DATE: [date]
CURRENT BOOKING SETUP: [what they're using now — phone, WhatsApp, Fresha, Calendly, nothing]
KEY PAIN POINT: [the one thing they most want to fix — use their exact words if possible]
BUSINESS TYPE: [industry and team size]
SERVICES: [rough count and duration range — e.g. "6 services, 30–90 min"]
STAFF NEEDING ACCESS: [number]
PAYMENT MODEL: [deposit / full upfront / pay on arrival / mix]
CONTENT READINESS:
  - Logo: [yes — ready to send / yes — needs finding / no — needs creating]
  - Photos: [professional / smartphone / stock only / none]
  - Copy: [they will write / I will write / mix]
PAGES BEYOND STANDARD: [any additional pages beyond Home, About, Services, Booking, Contact]
TIMELINE: [when they want to go live — or "exploratory, no deadline"]
DECISION MAKER: [just them / partner or manager also involved]
DEAL VALUE: [£995 setup + £99/month — or introductory £495 if applicable]
FLAGS: [anything unusual — scope risk, missing assets, feature gaps, low tech comfort, price sensitivity]
NEXT STEP: [proposal by X date]
```

**Using their exact words for the pain point matters.** When you open the proposal email with a sentence that mirrors how they described the problem, it signals immediately that you were listening. "You mentioned you spend two hours every Monday morning answering booking calls" lands far better than "you said managing bookings is difficult."

---

## Step 3 — Make the Go / No-Go Call

Before you write the proposal, take 30 seconds to ask yourself honestly: is this a good fit?

**Green flags — proceed:**
- Clear pain that your system directly solves
- Budget expectation is realistic (they did not recoil at the pricing)
- Business is established — has real customers, has been running at least a year
- Content and assets are either ready or manageable
- Timeline is realistic

**Amber flags — proceed with caution:**
- Price sensitivity was obvious — they may push back on the proposal
- Content is thin — no logo, no photos, expecting you to write everything
- Feature requests that are beyond your current build
- Decision maker is not on the call — someone else needs to sign off

**Red flags — consider whether to send a proposal at all:**
- Business is very new with no existing customer base
- Expectations are significantly misaligned with what you offer
- They need features you cannot deliver in Phase 1 and will not accept workarounds
- Very low tech comfort with no staff member who can help
- The call felt adversarial or the prospect was dismissive

If you have red flags, it is better to send a brief, honest reply than to invest time writing a proposal that is unlikely to convert:

> *"It was great to speak with you today. Based on our conversation, I want to be upfront — I think there might be a few things that aren't quite the right fit for where your business is right now. Specifically [one honest reason]. I'd rather tell you now than have you invest time reviewing a proposal that doesn't fully serve you. If things change or you'd like to revisit, I'm here."*

This costs you nothing and protects your time and reputation.

---

## Step 4 — Set the Proposal Task

In Bonsai, create a task: **"Send proposal — [Business Name]"** due tomorrow morning.

Do not leave it open-ended. A task without a due date does not get done.

If for any reason you cannot send the proposal tomorrow (you are unavailable, you need to check something first), set it for the earliest realistic date — and send the prospect a brief message:

> *"Great speaking with you today. I'll have the proposal with you by [date] — looking forward to putting it together."*

This keeps momentum and signals professionalism.

---

## Step 5 — Update Bonsai Status

Change the prospect's CRM status from **Qualified** to **Proposal Sent** at the point you actually send the proposal (Step 1.5) — not before.

If you have decided not to send a proposal after the Go / No-Go check, update the status to **Lost — Not a Fit** and add a brief note explaining why.

---

## Automation — Set Up Once

**Bonsai call summary template**
Paste the call summary template (shown in Step 2 above) into the notes field of every new prospect record the moment you create it at Step 1.1. It will sit there blank until you fill it in after the call. This removes the friction of formatting under time pressure post-call.

**Bonsai task reminder**
When you update a prospect's status to Qualified (discovery call booked), Bonsai should auto-create a task: *"Post-call — update notes and set proposal task."* Set this up as a task template in Bonsai once and it fires automatically every time.

---

## Step 1.4 — Checklist

- [ ] Jamie summary reviewed within 5 minutes of call ending
- [ ] All missing details added to Jamie manually if needed
- [ ] Bonsai call summary template filled in — all fields completed
- [ ] Prospect's exact words used for the key pain point where possible
- [ ] Go / No-Go check completed — decision made
- [ ] If No-Go: honest reply sent and status updated to Lost — Not a Fit
- [ ] If Go: proposal task created in Bonsai with specific due date
- [ ] Google Drive client folder confirmed as created
- [ ] Brief holding message sent to prospect if proposal will take longer than 24 hours

**Success measure:** Bonsai record is complete, the proposal task has a due date, and you could write the proposal right now if you had to — because everything you need is in Bonsai.
