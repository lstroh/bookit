## Context & Assumptions

This plan is built around the following confirmed constraints:

- **Product status:** Plugin mostly built, several features still missing
- **Sales assets:** Domain registered, nothing live yet
- **Time available:** 5–10 hours per week (full-time job alongside)
- **Budget to invest:** £1,000–3,000 before revenue comes in
- **Network:** Starting from scratch — no warm leads
- **Location:** Based in Wimbledon, SW London
- **Primary acquisition channels:** Direct outreach to local businesses + local Facebook/community groups
- **First client timeline:** Realistic target of Month 5

## The One Rule That Makes This Work

The single biggest risk for a solo operator with a full-time job is the product staying in permanent development mode — always one more feature away from being ready to show anyone.

**Personal rule to follow:** When the core booking flow works end-to-end for a single salon use case, the demo goes live and outreach starts — regardless of what else is still missing.

## Phase 1: Finish & Prepare (Months 1–3)

**Goal:** Reach a state where you could sign a client tomorrow if one appeared.

### Product

Identify which features are truly needed for the first client type (most likely a salon or therapist — the simplest use case) and park everything else as Phase 2. The plugin does not need full feature parity before moving forward. It needs to be functional enough that a real salon owner could use it without embarrassment.

Priority features for a salon MVP:
- End-to-end booking flow (service selection, calendar, confirmation)
- Staff assignment
- Email notifications (confirmation + reminder)
- Basic business dashboard (view and manage bookings)
- Stripe payment integration

Everything else (SMS, packages, recurring appointments, group bookings, intake forms) can follow in Phase 2.

**Infrastructure already in place (March 2026):**
- Hostinger Agency Startup hosting account: ✓ purchased
- wimbledonsmart.co.uk domain: ✓ registered
- wimbledonsmart.com domain: ✓ registered (redirect)

This removes one setup step from Phase 1 — hosting and domain are ready to use as soon as the Wimbledon Smart website is built.

### Sales Assets

Once the core booking flow works, build these two things in parallel:

**1. Demo Site**
A fake but realistic business — for example, "Shine & Style Salon, Wimbledon" — showing the full booking experience from the customer's perspective and the dashboard from the owner's perspective. This is the most powerful sales tool available and costs nothing but time. It must look real enough that a prospective client can picture their own business in it.

**2. Wimbledon Smart Website**
Five pages only:
- Home (who this is for and what problem it solves)
- How It Works (3–4 step overview of the process)
- Pricing (clear monthly and annual options, no hidden fees)
- Demo / Portfolio (link to the demo site, screenshots)
- Contact (simple form or direct email)

Keep it clean and professional. Built on WordPress. A premium theme or Elementor Pro is sufficient — this is not the main product, it's a brochure.

**Budget allocation for Phase 1:** ~£200–400 (reduced — hosting and domains already purchased)
- Hostinger Agency Startup: ✓ already purchased
- wimbledonsmart.co.uk + wimbledonsmart.com: ✓ already registered
- Theme / page builder: £100–200
- Business email (Google Workspace): £5/month

## Phase 2: Soft Launch & First Outreach (Months 3–5)

**Goal:** First conversation with a real potential client. Soft target: first paying client signed by end of Month 5.

Outreach starts when the demo site is live — not when the plugin is perfect.

### Facebook & Community Groups

Target: Wimbledon, Merton, SW19, SW20, Raynes Park local business groups.

Do not open with a sales pitch. Spend the first 4–6 weeks building a small presence:
- Answer questions about booking systems or website tools when they come up
- Comment helpfully on posts from local service businesses
- Share something genuinely useful once or twice (e.g. "I compared the top booking apps for London salons — here's what I found")

Make a direct ask only after establishing some presence. Budget 30–45 minutes per week. This compounds over time.

### Direct Outreach to Local Businesses

Target 20–30 local salons, therapists, and coaches in Wimbledon, Wimbledon Village, Raynes Park, and Colliers Wood.

Message approach — personal, not templated:
> "Hi [Name], I'm a local developer and I've built a booking system specifically for SW London service businesses. I'd love to show you a demo — 20 minutes over coffee or a screen share, completely no obligation. Happy to come to you."

Reference their specific business where possible. Aim for 5 conversations. Expect 1–2 genuinely interested.

### Introductory Pricing for First Two Clients

Offer the first two clients a meaningful discount in exchange for a testimonial and a written case study.

**Introductory offer:**
- Setup: £495 (instead of £995)
- Monthly: £99/month (standard rate)

This is not undervaluing the service — it is buying social proof, which at this stage is worth more than the extra £500 in setup revenue.

### Budget: Phase 2 (~£0–200)

| Item | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|
| Mostly time investment | £0 |
| Optional: small boosted Facebook post targeting local business owners in SW19/SW20 | £100–200 |
| **Total** | **~£0–200** |

## Phase 3: First Client Build & Refinement (Months 5–7)

**Goal:** Sign, build, and launch first client. Collect testimonial. Refine the delivery process.

The first client build will take longer than future ones — expect 30–40 hours including discovery, design, build, revisions, and onboarding. This is normal. Treat it as paid R&D.

What to learn from the first client:
- What features they actually use vs. what was assumed
- Where confusion or friction appears in the dashboard
- How much support they need in the first 30 days
- What language they use to describe their problems (valuable for future marketing copy)

### The Onboarding Process (Build This Once, Reuse Forever)

- 1-hour discovery call before build begins
- 30-minute onboarding call when site goes live
- Automated welcome email sequence (set up in Mailchimp free tier):
  - Day 0: Welcome + how to get started
  - Day 2: Have you added your services yet?
  - Day 5: Ready to test your first booking?
  - Day 30: Here's how to read your reports

### Key Milestone

After the first client is live and happy, everything needed to accelerate is in place: a real testimonial, a real case study, and a real example to show prospects. This is the inflection point of the business.

### Budget: Phase 3 (~£0)

Time investment only. No additional spend required at this stage.

## Phase 4: Scale to 5 Clients (Months 7–12)

**Goal:** 4–6 paying clients by end of Month 12. Monthly recurring revenue of £400–600/month.

With one happy client and a case study in hand, conversion rates on outreach roughly double.

### Growth Actions

**Referrals**
Ask the first client directly for introductions to other business owners they know. Offer one month free for any referred client who signs up. This is the highest-conversion channel available.

**Case Study**
Publish the first client case study on the Wimbledon Smart website. Include: the problem they had before, what was built, and a specific result (e.g. "Sarah now receives 80% of her bookings online and hasn't answered a booking call in three weeks"). This does more conversion work than any other page on the site.

**LinkedIn**
Begin a modest presence. Not daily posting — 2–3 posts per month sharing what you're learning and building. SW London service business owners are on LinkedIn. Topics that work well: booking system comparisons, no-show statistics for salons, time-saving tips for small service businesses.

**Small Google Ads test**
Once 2–3 clients are live, test a narrow Google Ads campaign. Target highly specific search terms: "booking system for salons London", "online booking for therapists UK", "website with booking for small business London". Keep budget tight and monitor closely.
